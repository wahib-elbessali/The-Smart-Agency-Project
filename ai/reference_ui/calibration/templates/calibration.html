calibrate, then verify before trusting it

Pick a camera tab, click 4 points that trace a shape you're confident is a right angle in real life (a floor tile, a rug, perpendicular wall/floor lines) -- ANY rectangle works, not just a square: the true aspect ratio is inferred from the 4 points' own perspective geometry, no typing, no numbers. The compute step will tell you whether that inference was confident or fell back to assuming square -- if it's not confident, dragging the auto-placed 4th corner to its true position (see the "calibrate" mode hint above) is the more reliable fix. Repeat per camera, then "compute + save calibration" writes site_calibration.json.

The 4-point fit's reprojection error is always ~0 regardless of whether the right-angle assumption actually holds -- that only confirms the math ran, not that it's correct. Two independently-calibrated cameras can each look perfect on their own and still disagree by metres about where the same real person is, because each one invented its own private floor coordinate system. Two ways to deal with that:

align cameras first -- actually fixes it. For when no single real rectangle is visible in every camera you want to fuse (common with wide baselines), so the calibrate step above can't anchor them to a shared frame by itself. Click the same real point across whichever cameras happen to see it, "record shared point", repeat for as many points/cameras as you like (any point, any subset -- you don't need the same set of cameras every time). "align cameras now" solves for the scale+rotation+translation that reconciles every camera's invented frame with a shared one (chaining through a third camera if two never directly share a point), corrects and re-saves the calibration. Needs >=2 shared points between any two cameras you want tied together.

cross-check -- read-only, and only usable on cameras that are already aligned (two freshly-calibrated cameras almost always "fail" a cross-check by construction, since nothing tied their frames together yet -- that's not new information). Click the same real point in 2+ aligned cameras and compare, ideally with different points than the ones you used to align. Units are "pt", an arbitrary made-up scale (1 pt = whatever real distance your calibration rectangle's side happened to be) -- NOT real metres or cm, on purpose, since that was never actually measured. A fraction of a pt apart means they still agree; a pt or more means something's still wrong. Changes nothing.

finding a shared point when the floor looks the same everywhere -- both cross-check and align let each camera's view scroll through its OWN video independently (cameras aren't necessarily time-synced). If the floor tiling is too uniform/repetitive to tell one corner from another, scrub each camera to a moment where a real person is standing in the shared area and click their feet instead -- a specific, identifiable landmark the floor itself doesn't provide.

align cameras (shared line) -- an alternative to shared points, for when a real straight edge (a wall/floor seam, a doorframe) is easier to identify precisely than one exact point. Click as many points along it as you're confident in, per camera (2 minimum, no maximum, doesn't need to match count across cameras) -- each click gets you live feedback: does THIS camera's own homography still see those points as collinear (tests that camera in isolation), and once 2+ cameras have points on the line, do they agree on its real length (tests alignment, using the fact that a rigid real line has ONE length regardless of which camera measures it). "finish this line" banks it; if the point counts matched across cameras, they're fed into the exact same alignment fit "shared point" uses, just with more, better-conditioned correspondences at once. No real-world measurement involved -- the unit ("pt") is intentionally made up, not cm or metres, so there's nothing to mismeasure.

mark entry gate -- separate from calibration itself: click real entry/exit points (a doorway, wherever people actually walk in from) in any calibrated camera's view. One click, saved immediately (gates.json, next to the video footage by default -- see --gates-out) -- no undo, no compute step, no multi-camera agreement needed (a gate point only needs ONE camera's Hinv to convert to world coordinates). Not required for tracking to work at all, but a tracker that knows real entry locations can tell "a genuinely new person just walked in" apart from "an existing person's position estimate drifted outside the association gate" -- the latter is far more likely if the point in question is nowhere near any marked gate.

view BEV overlay -- read-only, checks alignment directly by SIGHT instead of trusting diagnostics alone: every checked camera's frame gets rectified onto one shared floor-plane canvas and averaged. Correct calibration+alignment means a real floor feature (a rug edge, a tile seam) lands in the same spot from every camera and looks sharp; misalignment shows up as visible ghosting/doubling. The same check "align cameras now" already runs automatically (and reports here as "coverage check") -- this mode is for actually SEEING what a flagged camera's mismatch looks like, and for isolating which camera it is by unchecking the rest.
